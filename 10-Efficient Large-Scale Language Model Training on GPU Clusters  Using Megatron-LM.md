# Efficient Large-Scale Language Model Training on GPU Clusters Using Megatron-LM

Date: 2026-09-16

- [Link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.04473)

- Problem: Training large models requires substantial time and resources, and their training states may exceed the memory capacity of a single GPU or multi-GPU server. Distributing training introduces communication overhead, while pipeline parallelism introduces pipeline bubbles, during which GPUs are idle.
- Solution: They propose PTD-P, which integrates pipeline, tensor, and data parallelism to train large models efficiently across GPU clusters while preserving synchronous optimizer updates. For pipeline parallelism, they propose an interleaved 1F1B schedule that assigns multiple model chunks to each device.
- Claimed technical contributions:
  - They propose an interleaved pipeline schedule that reduces pipeline bubbles while maintaining a memory footprint comparable to non-interleaved 1F1B. The paper claims throughput improvements of 10+%, although the gains depend on batch size and communication overhead.
  - They analyze the interactions between parallelism types, microbatch size, and activation recomputation, providing analytical models and empirical results for their effects on pipeline bubbles, communication volume, memory consumption, and throughput.
  - They implement a scatter/gather communication optimization that avoids sending replicated activations redundantly between nodes. Each tensor-parallel GPU sends a different activation chunk, and the receiving GPUs reconstruct the full activation through a local all-gather.
  - They implement fused kernels for operations such as bias addition and GeLU, and attention scaling, masking, and softmax, reducing intermediate memory traffic and execution overhead.
  - They suggest heuristics for large-scale training: generally keep tensor parallelism within a node because of its frequent communication, use pipeline parallelism across nodes to accommodate larger models, and use data parallelism to scale training further once each distributed replica fits in memory.
- Metrics / Baselines:
  - They evaluated GPT models ranging from 1.7 billion to approximately 1 trillion parameters on up to 3,072 NVIDIA A100 GPUs. Each node contains eight 80-GB GPUs connected through NVLink/NVSwitch, with InfiniBand connections between nodes. They measured iteration runtime and calculated throughput using the estimated FLOP count, including activation recomputation.
  - They demonstrated weak scaling by increasing model size, GPU count, and batch size. The trillion-parameter configuration achieves approximately 163 TFLOP/s per GPU and 502 PFLOP/s in aggregate, corresponding to 52% of theoretical peak throughput (Table 1). The approximately 84-day full-training duration is projected from measured throughput and an assumed 450 billion training tokens.
  - They compared PTD-P with ZeRO-3 without model parallelism on 175B- and 530B-parameter models. At selected configurations, PTD-P achieves approximately 70% higher throughput and scales more effectively as GPU count increases with a fixed global batch size. The comparison does not evaluate ZeRO-3 combined with tensor or pipeline parallelism (Table 2 and Figure 10).
  - They compared interleaved and non-interleaved 1F1B schedules on a 175B-parameter model using 96 GPUs. Interleaving with the scatter/gather optimization improves throughput, particularly at smaller batch sizes; its advantage narrows as larger batches reduce the relative pipeline bubble (Figure 12).
  - They evaluated different combinations of tensor, pipeline, and data parallelism, along with microbatch sizes and activation recomputation. These experiments demonstrate tradeoffs between communication, GPU utilization, activation memory, and pipeline bubbles rather than a universally optimal configuration (Figures 13–17).
  - They measured the effects of implementation optimizations. Scatter/gather improves throughput by up to 11% in the tested configuration, while operator fusion improves throughput by 19% for the 175B model and 11% for the 530B model (Figure 18 and Section 5.8).
- Key insight and enabling idea: Different forms of parallelism have different communication patterns and should be matched to the hardware hierarchy. Frequent tensor-parallel communication benefits from fast connections within a node, while pipeline parallelism distributes layers across nodes and data parallelism distributes training examples across replicas. Interleaving smaller model chunks reduces pipeline bubbles, and scatter/gather reduces redundant cross-node transfers that would otherwise limit its benefits.
- Novelty and impact: The paper’s novelty lies in the interleaved 1F1B schedule and its systematic analysis and integration of pipeline, tensor, and data parallelism. Combined with communication and kernel optimizations, this approach demonstrates trillion-parameter training iterations across thousands of GPUs and provides practical guidance for configuring large-scale training.
- Technical Qualities:
  - Framing and assumptions: The paper clearly frames efficient training as a balance between memory capacity, communication overhead, GPU utilization, and pipeline bubbles while preserving synchronous optimizer updates. Its pipeline analysis assumes balanced stages and simplifies communication costs. The implementation and evaluation focus on repeated Transformer blocks and a cluster with high-bandwidth connections within and between nodes.
  - Merits of the technical contributions: The interleaved schedule reduces pipeline bubbles while retaining memory-efficient 1F1B execution. The scatter/gather optimization addresses its increased communication demands by exploiting replicated activations within tensor-parallel groups. The analytical models and experiments explain how parallelism degrees, microbatch size, and activation recomputation interact, supporting practical configuration decisions.
  - Does the evaluation support claims and reveal limitations of the proposed approach?: The evaluation supports the throughput and scaling claims through measured training iterations, comparisons of parallel configurations and schedules, and optimization ablations. It also reveals that interleaving and recomputation are not uniformly beneficial. However, the trillion-parameter full-training duration is estimated rather than demonstrated, and model-quality or convergence results are not reported. The weak-scaling experiments change model and batch sizes, the ZeRO-3 baseline excludes model parallelism, and the high-bandwidth hardware limits how directly the reported gains generalize to other configurations.
- Criticism:
  - The approximately 84-day estimate for full trillion-parameter training is extrapolated from measured iteration throughput and an assumed training-token count. The paper does not demonstrate a completed training run at that scale.
  - Model-quality and convergence results are not reported, so the evaluation establishes training throughput more directly than time to a target model quality.
  - The headline weak-scaling experiments increase model and batch sizes along with GPU count. These results demonstrate the ability to handle larger workloads but do not establish equivalent scaling benefits for a fixed model and global batch size.
  - Different tuning experiments use different model sizes and architectures. Although this accommodates memory constraints and isolates tradeoffs within individual experiments, systematically repeating the analyses across multiple model configurations would strengthen the generality of the proposed heuristics.
  - The ZeRO-3 comparison excludes tensor and pipeline parallelism from the baseline. Comparing against ZeRO-3 combined with model parallelism would better establish whether PTD-P’s advantages persist against a broader set of optimized configurations.
  - The evaluation uses a cluster with high-bandwidth connections within and between nodes. Experiments with lower bandwidth or different network topologies would clarify how the placement heuristics, interleaved scheduling benefits, and overall scaling depend on the hardware.
