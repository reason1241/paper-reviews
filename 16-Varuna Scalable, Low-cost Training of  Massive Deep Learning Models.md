# Varuna: Scalable, Low-cost Training of  Massive Deep Learning Models

Date: 2026-09-27

- [Link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.04007)

- Problem: Existing systems for training massive deep learning models require specialized, dedicated clusters with hundreds or thousands of GPUs connected by high-bandwidth interconnects, increasing training costs and limiting resource flexibility.
- Solution: The authors propose Varuna, a new system using commodity spot VMs and commodity networking for massive deep learning model training. It combines pipeline and data parallelism with communication-efficient scheduling and dynamically reconfigures the training job as GPU availability changes.
- Claimed technical contributions:
  - The authors claim that Varuna is the first system capable of training massive deep learning models on spot VMs with commodity networking. It combines data parallelism and pipeline parallelism, reducing dependence on specialized high-bandwidth interconnects. They argue and demonstrate that pipeline partitioning can outperform intra-layer partitioning in their evaluated commodity-network and hypercluster settings.
  - They propose a micro-batch schedule that interleaves forward passes, backward passes, and activation recomputation to reduce pipeline stalls and activation memory usage while preserving synchronous SGD semantics. The static schedule starts recomputation early enough to prepare activations for the corresponding backward task, waits for that task once recomputation finishes, and prioritizes backward when both forward and backward are ready. At runtime, Varuna can deviate from the static schedule and execute another ready task when network delays prevent the scheduled backward task from starting. This opportunistic scheduling is designed to improve tolerance to network latency and jitter compared with GPipe.
  - Job morphing dynamically reconfigures the training job according to available resources while preserving the global mini-batch size and synchronous update semantics through gradient accumulation. Varuna performs one-time calibration of forward and backward compute times, activation and gradient transfer times within and across nodes, and gradient all-reduce times. These measurements feed an event-driven simulator that estimates mini-batch execution time for candidate pipeline depths, data-parallel replica counts, and mappings of model sections to stages under the available GPU count. Varuna checkpoints each layer at consistent mini-batch boundaries every few mini-batches, allowing recovery and repartitioning after preemption. Its manager detects slow machines and excludes them from replica placement, monitors cluster availability, triggers morphing, and periodically attempts to acquire additional VMs.
  - They implement a non-intrusive PyTorch interface with automatic partitioning and cross-partition dependency detection. Varuna profiles compute times and activation sizes to identify candidate cut-points, using compute time to shortlist boundaries and choosing those with small activation sizes to maintain a high compute-communication ratio. At runtime, it selects a subset of these boundaries to form pipeline stages according to resource availability; unselected boundaries become pass-through points. It also identifies parameters reused across partition boundaries and marks them as shared. A tracer supported by modifications to PyTorch performs a training dry run, tags tensors by partition, and flags functions using tensors from multiple partitions, as well as untagged tensors treated as common. Users can mark relevant flagged tensors as shared so Varuna synchronizes them during training.
- Metrics / Baselines:
  - The authors evaluate BERT-large and GPT-2-style models ranging from 2.5B to 200B parameters on up to 300 Azure spot V100 GPUs with 10-Gbps Ethernet. They also use a 256-GPU DGX-2 hypercluster with NVLink within nodes and 200-Gbps InfiniBand between nodes. Main metrics include examples/s/GPU and useful TFLOP/s/GPU, excluding recomputation from the useful-work count.
  - Baselines include fully data-parallel BERT-large and Megatron with intra-layer partitioning for larger models. With a global batch size of 8192, Varuna achieves approximately 18× and 4.1× higher throughput per GPU than Megatron on commodity VMs for the 8.3B and 2.5B models, respectively (Figures 5–6). For the 8.3B model, Varuna on spot VMs achieves approximately 5.85× better cost-performance than Megatron on the hypercluster.
  - Pipeline comparisons include GPipe, DeepSpeed, Megatron-1F1B, and PipeDream. On single-GPU spot VMs with batch size 2400, Varuna achieves approximately 20–26% higher throughput than DeepSpeed and 13–14% higher throughput than Megatron-1F1B, with tensor parallelism and ZeRO disabled; PipeDream runs out of memory (Table 6). Measured GPipe comparisons use BERT-72 within one node, while multi-node network comparisons are simulated (Table 5).
  - They demonstrate execution of a 200B model on 102 GPUs with CPU-resident optimizer state, achieving 27.3 useful TFLOP/s/GPU. This demonstrates training throughput at that scale rather than a completed convergence run (§7.1.1).
  - Simulator estimates are generally within approximately 5% of measured mini-batch times (Table 7). A 60-hour 2.5B training run demonstrates job morphing under changing spot availability: aggregate throughput varies approximately fivefold, while per-GPU throughput varies by about 15% (Figure 8).
  - For convergence, they train the 2.5B model with batch size 8192 and match the number of training examples used by a reported Megatron baseline with batch size 512. Varuna achieves comparable model quality, including WikiText103 perplexity of 12.78 versus 12.76 (Figure 9 and §7.3).
- Key insight and enabling idea: Pipeline parallelism communicates less frequently and transfers substantially less data than intra-layer parallelism, making it suitable for commodity networks. However, pipeline depth must balance pipeline bubbles against data-parallel gradient synchronization costs. Varuna combines micro-batching and opportunistic scheduling to overlap communication with computation and tolerate network jitter. Predictable training operations enable reusable profiling and simulation-based configuration, while gradient accumulation allows the job to adapt to changing GPU availability without changing the global mini-batch size or synchronous update semantics.
- Novelty and impact: Varuna enables massive-model training on commodity spot VMs without requiring dedicated hyperclusters. Its novelty lies in combining communication-efficient pipeline scheduling with profiling-based automatic configuration and correctness-preserving job morphing across pipeline and data parallelism. This reduces dependence on specialized infrastructure, allows training to use changing spare GPU capacity, and lowers training costs while maintaining high throughput in the evaluated settings.
- Technical Qualities:
  - Framing and assumptions: The paper frames massive-model training as a balance between memory capacity, communication overhead, pipeline efficiency, and changing resource availability. Reactive job morphing and periodic checkpointing allow training to continue on preemptible resources while preserving the global mini-batch size and synchronous update semantics. The approach relies on predictable execution costs for reusable calibration, feasible model partitions on the remaining GPUs, and manageable checkpointing and recovery overhead. Its efficiency also depends on having enough micro-batches to amortize pipeline bubbles.
  - Merits of the technical contributions: Communication-efficient pipeline partitioning and opportunistic scheduling reduce network stalls, enabling resilient training on commodity spot VMs. Profiling-based simulation selects configurations without repeatedly running full training jobs, while job morphing adapts both pipeline and data parallelism to changing resources. Automatic partitioning and dependency detection reduce programming burden and help preserve training correctness. The evaluated configurations also outperform selected baselines on hyperclusters, showing benefits beyond access to cheaper resources.
  - Does the evaluation support claims and reveal limitations of the proposed approach?: Throughput comparisons, simulator validation, a 60-hour dynamic training run, and 2.5B-model convergence results support the approach in the tested settings. However, the 200B experiment uses micro-batch size one and CPU-resident optimizer state and does not demonstrate convergence. The large-global-batch convergence experiment covers one model configuration and does not establish universal model-quality preservation. Simulator validation does not report repeated-trial variability or confidence intervals, limiting assessment of prediction stability. The evaluation also does not systematically test changing background network congestion or quantify checkpointing, recovery, and lost-work costs across different preemption rates. Some GPipe network comparisons are simulated, and the pipeline comparisons disable tensor parallelism and ZeRO.
- Criticism:
  - The 200B experiment demonstrates training throughput with micro-batch size one and CPU-resident optimizer state, but does not demonstrate convergence or final model quality.
  - The 2.5B model’s large-global-batch convergence experiment covers one model configuration and does not establish universal model-quality preservation.
  - Simulator validation does not report repeated-trial variability or confidence intervals, limiting assessment of prediction stability.
  - The evaluation does not systematically test changing background network congestion or quantify checkpointing, recovery, and lost-work costs across different preemption rates.
  - Some multi-node GPipe comparisons are simulated rather than measured, limiting direct evidence under real network conditions.
  - Comparisons with DeepSpeed and Megatron-1F1B isolate pipeline efficiency by disabling tensor parallelism and optimizations such as ZeRO. Additional comparisons against fully tuned combinations would clarify whether Varuna’s advantages persist at the complete-system level.
  - The paper does not describe or evaluate recovery from failure of the dedicated Varuna manager, leaving the availability of its management component unclear.
